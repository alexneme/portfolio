# Day 2 – What Does a DevOps Engineer Do?

![DevOps Engineer Image](/images/devops-engineer.jpeg)

Yesterday (Day 1) we talked about DevOps in general. Today it’s time to dig a little deeper: what does a DevOps Engineer actually do?  

Think of it this way: there are **two big parts in any application**:  
- **Dev** → the people who write and test the code.  
- **Ops** → the ones who put it online and keep it running.  

DevOps is exactly the **bridge between these two worlds**.  

---

### Where do you fit in?

You won’t be coding the application itself, but you need to **understand how it’s built**: which languages, tools, and processes are involved. That knowledge is key to getting it up and running on the other side — on the server or in the cloud.  

And of course, knowing the basics of Linux, networking, containers, and infrastructure will be essential. You don’t need to be a specialist in everything, but having a good sense of how the pieces connect makes all the difference.  

---

### The role of DevOps

On a daily basis, a DevOps Engineer helps answer questions like:  
- How do we deploy the new version of the app?  
- How do we do it in a **continuous, automated, and tested** way?  
- How do we make sure servers, containers, and services all work together smoothly?  

In short, you’ll be orchestrating this symphony of code, infrastructure, and automation.  

---

### Wrapping up

- Dev creates.  
- Ops maintains.  
- DevOps connects and automates.  

That’s what we’ll be exploring throughout the challenge. Today was just a high-level overview, but enough to feel the scope (and the fun) of the role.  

Tomorrow, we’ll keep digging even deeper.
