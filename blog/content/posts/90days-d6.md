# Day 6 – DevOps: Real Stories

![DevOps Team Image](/images/devopsteam.jpg)

At first, DevOps seemed out of reach — something only giants like Netflix or Fortune 500 companies could do. But today, things have changed: more and more businesses across different industries are adopting DevOps practices and seeing real results.  

The key takeaway is simple: **when done right, DevOps speeds up software delivery and improves quality**.  

Today, let’s look at companies that transformed their operations with DevOps and see the impact it had.  

---

## Amazon  

In 2010, Amazon migrated its physical servers to AWS. This allowed them to scale resources up and down in tiny increments, saving both time and money.  

By 2011, Amazon had implemented continuous deployment: developers could deploy code whenever they wanted, to whichever servers they needed. The result? A new deployment every **11.6 seconds on average**!  

---

## Netflix  

Who hasn’t used Netflix? The user experience is outstanding, and that doesn’t happen by accident.  

Netflix built a highly automated model: developers package code into deployable web images that go straight into production without depending on manual IT operations.  
If something fails, the system automatically rolls back and routes traffic to the previous version.  

The secret? **Speed, flexibility, and continuous monitoring**.  

---

## Etsy  

Etsy faced what many of us know well: slow, painful deployments and siloed teams.  

Around 2009, they started letting developers deploy their own code. That was a game changer: once developers became responsible for deployment, they also felt responsible for performance, uptime, and reliability.  

This highlights one of the core values of DevOps: a **learning culture**. Even failures can be successes if they come with lessons.  

Check out the full interview with Michael Rembetsy, Etsy’s VP of Technical Operations, on how he put the DevOps ideology into practice: [Full interview](https://blog.neme.site/posts/interview-etsy)

---

## Other Takeaways  

- DevOps unites **development and operations** into a single flow.  
- The focus is to **shorten the delivery cycle**, release frequent improvements, and stay aligned with business goals.  
- More than tools, DevOps is a **mindset of continuous delivery**: build, test, deploy, operate, monitor — and repeat.
